# Workflow prioritization

- Status: accepted for the build (2026-09-26). The human confirmed the four-workflow scope before this phase and pre-approved the plan; the order below is open to the team's review, and the weights may be adjusted later as a new pre-registration version.
- Evidence: [`docs/analysis/workflow-scores.md`](../analysis/workflow-scores.md) and [`docs/analysis/workflow-evidence.md`](../analysis/workflow-evidence.md), generated by `make analysis` from the full organizer delivery (`organizer-v1.0.0-2026-08-31`, 686,296 interactions and 67,095 complaints, 2023-06-17 to 2026-06-17).
- Method: [`docs/analysis/workflow-scoring-preregistration.md`](../analysis/workflow-scoring-preregistration.md), committed before any score was computed ([ADR 0023](../adr/0023-workflow-prioritization-method.md)).

This document does not select or drop workflows. The scope is `account_inquiry`, `card_support`, `dispute`, and `credit` (CLAUDE.md section 1). It orders the build and depth work and classes the sub-intents inside each workflow.

## Decision

**Build and depth order: `account_inquiry`, then `card_support`, then `dispute`, then `credit`.** Every workflow meets the same depth bar; the order says which is built first as the reference implementation of the shared engine, which gets evaluation depth first, and which is the first cut candidate if the deadline forces one (CLAUDE.md section 1: cut back to clarify, abstain, or hand off, documented, never shallow).

| Rank | Workflow | Score (0 to 100) | Weight sensitivity range | Rank range over 12 weight variants | Why here |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `account_inquiry` | 70.4 | 68.8 to 72.2 | 1 to 1 | Largest and only unambiguous demand (31.9% of contacts), high data support (92%), lowest harm (read only), highest proxy automatable share (70%). The safest base for the engine, grounding verifier, and as-of answers |
| 2 | `card_support` | 64.4 | 62.6 to 66.0 | 2 to 3 | Second in demand under the primary mapping (20.0%), best data support (98%), and the first verified write (protective block with step-up and read-back). Tied with `dispute` within the 2-point margin; the pre-registered tiebreak (data support) puts it first |
| 3 | `dispute` | 63.5 | 61.5 to 65.2 | 2 to 3 | The highest pain by far (first contact resolution 43.6%, CSAT 1 or 2 on 54.5%, handle time 435 s) and full demo depth, held back by the weakest data support (32%): complaints link to no transaction and name another customer's product |
| 4 | `credit` | 55.8 | 53.2 to 58.8 | 4 to 4 | Lowest demand (7.3%, and only through an assumed mapping), highest harm (regulatory and fair-lending exposure), no forward-looking risk label in the data. High pain and full demo depth do not offset these |

The first and last places are robust: `account_inquiry` is first and `credit` last in all 12 weight variants and in all three mapping scenarios except the alternative one, where `Producto` maps to `credit` and `credit` moves to second. The middle is not robust: `card_support` and `dispute` swap when the data support weight drops by 5 points (1 of 12 variants), and are 0.9 points apart.

## Sub-intents: automate, clarify, or hand off

Classes follow the pre-registered rule (hand off at capability 0; clarify first when sub-intent data support is below 0.5; otherwise automate, ordered by sub-intent score). No sub-intent falls below the data support threshold, so no sub-intent is classed "clarify first" as a whole; the clarifying paths below are the parts the records cannot settle, with their measured size.

| Workflow | Automate, in order | Clarify or abstain inside the flow (evidence) | Hand off |
|---|---|---|---|
| `account_inquiry` | 1 `balance_inquiry`, 2 `payment_status`, 3 `statement_request` | Statement totals: transfers and adjustments carry no direction, 23.2% of transactions, so totals report them as unclassified; document or delivery requests abstain with a clause | Human request, distress, legal mention, repeated failure |
| `card_support` | 1 `card_status`, 2 `card_block` | Several cards and an unclear choice: ask which card; 4.9% of cards have no expiry date, so expiry answers abstain for them | `card_unblock_request`, `card_replacement_request` (by design: identity and fraud checks the prototype cannot verify) |
| `dispute` | 1 `dispute_status`, 2 `dispute_new` (intake only; the investigation stays human) | Transaction identification: no complaint names a transaction and none names the customer's own product, so intake must confirm the transaction with the customer (the ambiguous-transaction path); 67% of dispute complaints carry no claimed amount | Amount above the automatic limit, repeat complainer (14.9% of dispute complaints), legal mention, verification mismatch |
| `credit` | 1 `credit_application_status`, 2 `credit_product_info`, 3 `credit_application` (intake for human review), 4 `credit_eligibility` (indicative only) | 15.0% of customers lack a credit score and 20.0% lack income (32.0% lack one or both), so eligibility asks for the missing fact or returns `insufficient_data` or `review_required`; mortgages are information only | Borderline or contested results, products that need a human assessment. A lending decision is never automated |

## Breadth risk

The brief rewards depth, not breadth, and the team chose four workflows anyway. The evidence says plainly where that is weakest.

| Workflow | Demand, primary | Demand, strict (unambiguous evidence only) | Data support | Automatable, proxy | Main weakness |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `account_inquiry` | 31.9% | 31.9% | 92% | 70.1% | Demo depth: no write action, so it cannot show a verified write |
| `card_support` | 20.0% | **0%** | 98% | 68.6% | Its whole demand rests on reading `Producto` as card servicing; no complaint category and no reliable product reference supports it |
| `dispute` | 19.1% | 3.6% (charge complaints) | **32%** | 33.2% | Records cannot link a complaint to a transaction, so the learned transaction resolver has no ground truth in the organizer data and must rely on silver labels of measured precision (phase 10) |
| `credit` | 7.3% | **0%** | 70% | 50.0% | Lowest demand, highest harm, and no forward-looking risk label (one snapshot) |

**The weakest candidate for depth is `credit`**: last in every weight variant, the smallest demand even under its favorable mapping reading, and the highest harm. If the deadline forces a cut, `credit` is cut first to catalog information plus a documented handoff, then `card_support`, whose demand evidence is the most assumption-dependent. `dispute` should not be cut despite its weak data support, because it carries the highest measured pain and the learned component the brief asks to benchmark.

## Evidence that would change the order

| New evidence | Effect |
|---|---|
| A finer contact reason, or a team-labeled set of interactions, showing `Producto` contacts are mostly about credit products | The alternative scenario: `credit` rises to second (64.0), `card_support` falls to last (27.8) |
| Silver-label matching (phase 10) that links complaints to transactions with measured precision | `dispute` data support rises (the link item is one of its four). Above about 0.52, for example a link with 80% precision, its score clears `card_support` by more than the tie margin and it becomes second |
| Human automatable-share labels (pending) | Replace the proxy for any workflow with at least 75 matching adjudicated items. Because transcripts are two balance templates, only `account_inquiry` is likely to get enough; it would lose first place only if its labeled share fell below about 50% (against the 70% proxy) |
| The team lowers the data support weight by 5 points or more | `dispute` and `card_support` swap |
| Verified cost figures | No change: cost is reported, never scored |
| The organizer delivers a second snapshot of customers and products | `credit` data support rises (forward label available), about 3 points of score; not enough to leave last place alone |

## Risks

- **Mapping.** Three of four interaction mappings are assumptions; the strict scenario shows two workflows with no unambiguous interaction demand. Stated in every report and above.
- **Proxy automatable share.** The proxy measures historically simple contacts, not what the system can do. Human labels are pending and, for three workflows, cannot come from transcripts.
- **Synthetic artifacts.** Flat hour-of-day curves, constant escalation, flat complaint outcomes, constant sentiment on transactional contacts, and templated transcripts are generator properties; the analysis keeps them out of the scores where known.
- **Costs.** Projections from unverified team assumptions; the savings they imply are illustrations, not measurements.
- **Digital errors.** Error events are followed by a contact within 24 hours no more often than other events (0.43% against 0.42%, ratio 1.03): no evidence that digital failures drive contact demand in this data, and no causal claim either way.

## Consequences for later phases

- Phase 05 seeds data for all four workflows in the order above; phase 06 writes policy clauses in the same order.
- Phase 09 builds `account_inquiry` first as the reference state machine, then the card block write, then dispute intake, then credit.
- Phase 10 must not train or evaluate the router on organizer transcripts: utterances must be team-authored or model-paraphrased from team-authored seeds, labeled as such, in Spanish and Portuguese. The dispute resolver needs silver labels with measured precision because complaints name no transaction.
- Phase 14 reports every metric per workflow, with these historical baselines as the reference and the segment baselines of the evidence report for the disparity analysis.
